Why I made it

My aftermarket Android head unit is still too bright at night, even at its lowest setting. The dimmer apps I tried were either complicated to set up or full of ads.

I only need one switch and one slider. So that is all Car Dimmer does: it lays an adjustable dark layer over the screen, while maps and buttons underneath keep working.

Easy to use in the car

Darker than the minimum

A dark layer over the whole screen, adjustable in 5% steps. Navigation, music and other apps work as usual.

Big switch, big slider

Made for the car: large text and large controls, one glance and one tap. Side by side on wide screens, stacked on narrow ones.

Schedule

Like a night-light mode: dims at the start time and returns to normal at the end time. You can still switch it by hand, or turn the schedule off.

Starts again after boot

When the head unit restarts, Car Dimmer comes back with your last settings.

Switch from the notification

Install and set up

  1. Get the APK onto the head unitIt is not on Google Play. Open this page in the head unit’s browser, or download it on a computer or phone and move it over with a USB stick or Bluetooth. Allow installing unknown apps when asked.
  2. Allow “Display over other apps”Open Car Dimmer and tap “Allow” to switch on this permission, so the dark layer can be shown.
  3. Set the head unit’s own brightness to minimumLower the built-in brightness first, then let Car Dimmer cover the rest. This gives the best result.
  4. Turn on “Dim” and drag the sliderTo dim automatically after dark, turn on “Schedule” and set the start and end times.

Questions

Why does it stop at around 80%?

Since Android 12, if a layer drawn over other apps is too opaque, the apps underneath stop receiving touches (this prevents malicious overlays). The limit is set by the system, usually 80%, and the app shows the limit on your device.

That is why you should set the head unit’s own brightness to minimum first.

Why does it need “Display over other apps”?

Dimming works by drawing a dark layer on top of everything, and Android requires you to switch that permission on yourself. The layer is used only for dimming and does not intercept your touches.

Will it work on my head unit? On a phone?

Any head unit running Android 13 or later that lets you install apps, and phones and tablets too. The app was developed for an aftermarket FYT head unit (Android 13, 2000×1200); other brands are untested, and some head units restrict background apps or autostart. Let me know if you run into problems.

Most factory head units do not allow installing apps, and Android Auto and CarPlay are projections of your phone, so it cannot be used there.

Does it keep running in the background?

While dimming, a small foreground service keeps the layer in place and you will see a “Car Dimmer” notification. With dimming off and no schedule it is not needed.

Which languages does the app have?

English and Traditional Chinese. It follows the device language: Chinese devices show Chinese, everything else shows English.

A warning appears during installation?

Because the app is not installed from Google Play, Android asks you to allow “install unknown apps”. The source code is public, so you can check it yourself.
